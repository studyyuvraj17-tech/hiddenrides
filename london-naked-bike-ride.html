World Naked Bike Ride London

London’s annual World Naked Bike Ride is a peaceful public cycling demonstration highlighting cyclist vulnerability, oil dependence and car-centred streets while celebrating body freedom. The London ride has taken place annually since 2004.

What the ride stands for

The London ride is part of the worldwide WNBR movement. Its stated aims include protesting dependence on oil, challenging car culture, calling for better rights and safer conditions for cyclists, demonstrating cyclists’ vulnerability on city streets and celebrating body freedom.

How the London ride works

The 2026 edition brought together more than 1,000 riders across multiple feeder routes. Organised start points included Clapham Junction, Croydon, Deptford, Hackney Wick, Kew Bridge, Regent’s Park, Tower Hill and Wellington Arch, plus an accessible shorter option. Routes and start points can change each year.

Next edition: what is confirmed?

As of November 3, 2026, the organisers describe Sunday 13 June 2027 as a probable planning date, not a final confirmed event schedule. Check the official website before making travel plans; meeting points, timings and routes for 2027 may be announced later.

Plan responsibly

  • Read the organiser’s current participation, behaviour and photography guidance.
  • Respect consent and do not photograph or film people in ways that breach the event’s policy.
  • Bring a roadworthy bicycle, water and appropriate lights; follow marshals and traffic instructions.
  • Check local rules and the organiser’s latest updates before attending.
